1. What FEM Swap is

FEM Swap is a software interface for viewing token and pool information and preparing blockchain transactions. It is based on a fork of Uniswap V2, but may be modified. A V2-style automated market maker generally uses liquidity pools and smart contracts to facilitate token swaps; the contracts, supported networks, pool parameters, and fees actually used are those deployed and shown by FEM Swap, not necessarily those of Uniswap.

In the standard Uniswap V2 design, each pair contract holds reserves of two tokens and prices trades using a constant-product invariant. You need a compatible wallet and supported network to submit a transaction. Your wallet must be used to review and authorize each transaction. Transactions are generally public, may be irreversible once confirmed, and are subject to network congestion, fees, and the rules of the relevant blockchain.

A displayed quote, price, route, or estimated output can change before execution and is not a guarantee. Slippage, liquidity, token behavior, network fees, contract settings, and third-party systems may affect the final result. If FEM Swap uses a V2-style router, its swap call may enforce a minimum output and transaction deadline; check the actual transaction and settings presented by FEM Swap and your wallet. The blockchain network may charge gas even if a transaction fails.

Fees are distinct: the network charges gas; a pool may charge a trading fee; FEM may or may not charge an interface or service fee; and a protocol fee may depend on the deployed contracts and their configuration. The original Uniswap V2 pair code applies a 0.30% input fee in its swap invariant, but FEM’s fork may change this. The original factory also has a configurable protocol-fee mechanism. Neither upstream setting confirms the fees or recipients in FEM Swap. Review the app and transaction details before approving.
